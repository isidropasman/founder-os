### P1 — Pick one metric and let it kill the other work
Claim: A startup should choose a single primary metric for the current stage and use it to
decide what not to do. Multiple co-equal priorities mean no priorities.
Source: How to Set KPIs and Goals (YC Startup School) — paraphrase
Applies when: Founder is juggling several workstreams; weekly prioritization
Conflicts with: —

### P2 — Launch earlier than is comfortable
Claim: The MVP exists to start the learning loop, not to be good. Founders systematically
delay launch to add features that the market has not asked for.
Source: How to Plan an MVP (YC Startup School) — paraphrase
Applies when: Founder is adding scope before shipping; launch date keeps sliding
Conflicts with: —

### P3 — Build in short cycles with a decision at the end
Claim: Work in fixed short cycles that end in an explicit keep/kill decision informed by
user behavior, rather than open-ended builds evaluated on gut feel.
Source: Building Product (YC Startup School) — paraphrase
Applies when: Planning the week; deciding how long to give a bet before judging it
Conflicts with: —

### P4 — Founder time follows the constraint, including into discomfort
Claim: The founder's calendar should be dominated by whatever is currently the binding
constraint — usually sales or users early on — even when it is the part of the job they
like least.
Source: Building Product (YC Startup School) — paraphrase
Applies when: The plan avoids the founder's stated weak spots; time allocation questions
